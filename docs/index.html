Source Foundry

Source Foundry, a stealth chip manufacturing startup founded by Stanford researchers Abdulmalik Obaid and Joe Burg, received a $400 million investment from hedge fund Situational Awareness, bringing total funding to at least $500 million. The company's most recent fundraising was at a $5 billion valuation. Sequoia Capital is among the prior investors in the company. The startup is focused on reinventing chip manufacturing processes.

The round was one of three $1 billion-or-more financings closed in the same week. The company is building SMR technology intended to provide dedicated power capacity for data centers.

Base Power, an Austin-based home battery startup led by Zach Dell, raised $1 billion in a Series D round at a $13 billion post-money valuation. Top B2B Product Launches

Analyst Note: This was a notably thin week for B2B software launches — the article set is dominated by earnings roundups, personnel announcements, funding news, and tangential content with little direct relevance to product launches. The few genuine launches present are early-stage or niche plays with limited Tier-1 media coverage or measurable buzz. Rather than pad this list with three entries, only two launches clear a reasonable quality bar; the third slot would require crediting a press release-level drop with no meaningful launch execution.

Skan AI — Enterprise AI Platform

5/10
Company Skan AI is a Menlo Park-based process intelligence and AI automation company targeting enterprise operations teams. The $63M raise signals late-growth stage, though exact ARR and customer scale are not disclosed in available coverage.
The Launch Skan AI announced a new Enterprise AI Platform alongside a $63M funding round. The platform appears designed to bridge the gap between AI investment and measurable operational returns — connecting AI capabilities directly into enterprise business workflows rather than operating as a standalone analytics layer.
Product Fit This is a logical extension of Skan's existing process intelligence roots — moving from observation and mining into active AI-driven intervention. The "platform" framing suggests a bid to expand beyond point-solution positioning into a broader enterprise system of record for operational AI, which is a credible but crowded ambition.
Launch Playbook The launch was paired with a funding announcement — a classic momentum-doubling tactic that trades editorial attention on capital for product coverage. Coverage appeared on HPCwire/AIWire, a relevant trade vertical, but did not surface in Tier-1 outlets. No conference keynote, marquee customer co-announcement, or social amplification mechanic was evident from available sources.
Messaging The framing centers on closing the "AI execution gap" — the idea that enterprise AI investments are failing to produce measurable returns because AI remains disconnected from workflows. This is a legitimate and resonant narrative in 2026, but it is also extremely crowded territory. The product's differentiation relative to ServiceNow, UiPath, or Salesforce Agentforce is not clearly articulated in available coverage, which is a missed opportunity.
Why This Rating The funding-plus-launch combo is smart timing and the trade press pickup is real, but the absence of Tier-1 coverage, a clear competitive wedge in messaging, or any marquee customer announcement keeps this squarely in the "competent but unremarkable" range. A $63M raise deserved a more engineered launch moment — a keynote anchor, a lighthouse customer quote, or a specific capability demo that would give journalists a sharper hook.

SlashExperts — Carly AI Agent

4/10
Company SlashExperts is a San Francisco-based early-stage B2B startup positioning itself as an "AI-native trust layer for B2B sales," focused on automating customer advocacy and peer reference programs to influence deals in motion.
The Launch Carly is an autonomous AI agent that surfaces the right customer reference or advocate voice to the right buyer at the right stage of a sales cycle — automating what has historically been a manual, relationship-dependent process managed by customer success or sales teams. The value proposition: shorten sales cycles and lift win rates through AI-orchestrated social proof.
Product Fit Carly is a natural evolution of SlashExperts' existing customer-to-buyer matching infrastructure. The pivot from human-facilitated expert calls to an autonomous AI agent is a credible product progression, though it raises questions about whether the product's value was predicated on human authenticity that an AI agent may undermine.
Launch Playbook Distribution ran through GlobeNewswire with syndication to outlets including Manila Times — a wire-first approach that signals a limited PR budget and no Tier-1 media relationship at launch. No conference keynote, Product Hunt front page, or LinkedIn viral moment was evident. The announcement was self-contained with no co-launch customer or partner named.
Messaging The headline — "turns customers into your best sellers" — is punchy and benefit-led, which is above average for an early-stage B2B launch. The "attribution-ready revenue" framing is smart for a sales audience skeptical of advocacy ROI. However, the positioning risks conflating AI automation with human trust, and no proof points, case studies, or beta customer results are cited to ground the claims.
Why This Rating The product concept is genuinely interesting and addresses a real gap in B2B sales tooling, and the headline messaging shows PMM instincts above the early-stage norm. But the launch execution is almost entirely wire-dependent with no earned media, no social amplification, and no customer validation to build credibility — which matters doubly for a product whose entire premise is built on trust. A Product Hunt launch paired with even one named customer win would have meaningfully moved the needle.
